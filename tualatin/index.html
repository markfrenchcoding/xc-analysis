Identical arithmetic, three populations. The gap between them is why this page never lets a statistic choose its own sample.

Two rulers, same athletes

Track happens on a flat oval at a measured distance. Cross country happens on whatever the host mowed. This is what that difference is worth, measured on athletes who did both in the same school year.

Which is why a VDOT from track and a VDOT from cross country never share an axis here. The change between two years is comparable, because the offset cancels in a difference. The level is not.

Race any two seasons

Two squads of seven from this programme's own record, on one strip, at the pace each athlete averaged in that season. Scored as a dual meet: everyone takes a place, the first five a side add up, the sixth and seventh displace without scoring, lower total wins.

It is a pace projection, not a race. Everybody runs their season best at an even pace, so nobody passes anybody and the order is settled at the gun. What the strip shows is not who would win a Saturday — it is how far apart these two squads actually were.

Speed or strength

A 1,500m against a 3,000m, both on the track, so the comparison is clean. Read against the squad's own middle rather than against zero, because an offset everybody shares says nothing about anybody. This is a question about what somebody is built for, not about who is better.

Who already races together

Every pair of team-mates who have been on the same start line four times or more, by how close they finish. These groups exist whether anybody planned them or not.
